### Professional Judgement and Variation in Technique

Livaware acknowledges that competent clinicians may use different, evidence-informed techniques to achieve the same clinical outcome. Where an SOP allows more than one acceptable approach, you may use your professional judgement to select the most appropriate method, provided it remains within your scope of practice and competence.

If you vary from the default approach described in an SOP, you must record a clear, factual, contemporaneous and defensible rationale. Your documentation should explain what you did, why you did it, what you observed, and what you escalated or advised. Use Appendix C - Variance Documentation Prompt.

If an SOP conflicts with (a) the treating clinician's plan, (b) the prescription, (c) manufacturer IFU, or (d) local laboratory/pathology requirements, you must follow the higher-authority instruction and document the rationale and any escalation.


### Competency and Authorisation

No staff member may undertake an invasive procedure under this handbook without documented training, competency assessment, and authorisation within Livaware's governance process. Competency is procedure-specific and device-specific. See Appendix K for the full training and competency matrix.

## 1.0 The Livaware Way

This integrated framework of policies, procedures, and governance ensures that every member of the Livaware team is empowered and equipped to deliver the exceptional, safe, and compassionate care that is our hallmark.


### 1.1 Introduction to the Manual

This operations manual provides all Livaware staff—both clinical and non-clinical—with a single, integrated source of truth for procedures, professional conduct, and patient care standards. Its goal is to translate Livaware's core policies into actionable, daily guidance to ensure the highest levels of safety, quality, and consistency across all our services. Adherence to the principles and procedures outlined in this manual is essential for delivering the transformative, patient-centred care that defines Livaware.


### 1.2 Our Philosophy of Care

At Livaware, our philosophy of care is the 'why' behind every action and decision. It guides us to see care not as a series of tasks, but as a transformative process that empowers individuals, fosters human connection, and inspires hope. This philosophy is built upon a foundation of compassion, innovation, and deep respect for the uniqueness of every person we serve.

#### Our Core Beliefs

- Care that transforms lives: We view care as a process that empowers individuals to rediscover independence, purpose, and joy. Every interaction is an opportunity to inspire confidence and create lasting positive change.

- Seamless integration of care: We integrate clinical precision, functional health insights, and holistic practices into a personalised care ecosystem. This ensures every patient's experience is coordinated and seamless.

#### Our Four Guiding Principles

- Person-centred care: Every care plan begins with understanding the individual—their values, goals, and experiences. We meet people where they are and support them as they move forward.
- Empathy in action: Empathy is woven into every action. We strive to recognise the unspoken needs of those we serve and respond with meaningful support.
- Innovation with purpose: We are driven to deliver forward-thinking care, but every technological advancement we adopt is measured by its ability to enhance a person's life.
- Community and collaboration: We believe healthcare is a shared effort. We actively engage patients, families, and multidisciplinary teams to create unified, comprehensive care journeys tailored to complex needs.


### 1.3 Our Commitment to Governance

A strong governance framework is strategically essential for translating Livaware's philosophy into safe, effective, and compliant practice. This framework provides the structure and accountability necessary to meet and exceed the standards set by the Care Quality Commission (CQC). Our commitment to governance is rooted in our core values:

- Safety: Creating environments where risks are minimised for patients and staff.
- Empathy: Delivering care that understands and respects individual needs and values.
- Integrity: Maintaining transparency and accountability in all our practices and decisions.

## 3.0 Patient Engagement and Communication

### 3.1 Patient-Centred Communication and Information Sharing

Effective communication is the foundation of safe, coordinated, and patient-centred care at Livaware. This section outlines the standards for all interactions, ensuring clarity, confidentiality, and collaboration among patients, families, and multidisciplinary teams.

#### Five Core Principles of Communication

- Clarity and Accuracy: All communication must be precise, concise, and easily understandable, avoiding jargon.
- Confidentiality and Security: Patient information is handled in strict compliance with UK GDPR and other regulations.
- Respect and Sensitivity: Communication is tailored to individual preferences, needs, and cultural contexts.
- Timeliness: Information is shared promptly to enable effective, coordinated decision-making.
- Accountability: All staff are responsible for adhering to these communication standards and safeguarding information.

#### Modes of Communication

- In-Person: Engage actively using open body language and active listening. Confirm understanding by summarising key points.
- Written: Use accessible formats and clear, professional language.
- Digital: Share information securely via encrypted email or password-protected patient portals. Avoid discussing sensitive information on unprotected platforms.
- Telephone: Confirm the recipient's identity before sharing details and use summarisation to ensure mutual understanding.


### 3.2 Informed Consent and Advocacy

Informed consent is a fundamental patient right and the cornerstone of ethical care at Livaware. It is a process that respects an individual's autonomy and ensures their voice is heard and valued. This section provides the principles; detailed procedural guidance is in SOP 11 (Consent, Capacity, and Best Interests).

#### Four Core Principles

- Voluntary: Consent must be given freely, without coercion or undue influence.
- Informed: Patients must receive clear, accurate information about their care options, including the purpose, risks, benefits, and alternatives.
- Capacity: Patients must have the mental capacity to make the decision. If capacity is in question, assessments and safeguards are implemented in line with the Mental Capacity Act 2005.
- Right to Refuse: Patients have the absolute right to withhold or withdraw their consent at any time.

#### Obtaining Informed Consent

1. Provide Information: Offer clear, tailored information using accessible language and formats (e.g., large print, interpreters). Explain the purpose, risks, benefits, and alternatives.
2. Allow Time for Decision-Making: Ensure patients have sufficient time to consider their options and ask questions.
3. Document Consent: Record the information provided, the patient's decision, and any questions raised in the patient's record.
4. Renew Consent: Consent must be reviewed and renewed for ongoing treatments or if there are significant changes to the care plan or the patient's condition.

#### Special Considerations

- Children and Young People: Consent must be obtained from a parent or legal guardian for patients under 16, unless they are deemed "Gillick competent." Young people should always be engaged in discussions about their care.
- Patients Lacking Capacity: Capacity is assessed in line with the Mental Capacity Act 2005. Decisions must be made in the patient's best interests, involving family or advocates where possible, and the process must be thoroughly documented (see SOP 11 and Appendix E).
- Emergency Situations: If obtaining consent is not possible, necessary care may be provided in the patient's best interests. The rationale and actions taken must be documented as soon as is practicable.


### 3.3 Managing Patient Confidentiality

Patient confidentiality is a critical legal and ethical obligation at Livaware. It is the foundation of the trust that patients place in us. This section provides a practical framework for safeguarding all patient information in compliance with the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Caldicott Principles.

#### Core Principles

- Need-to-Know Access: All patient information is treated as strictly confidential and is accessed only by authorised staff on a need-to-know basis.
- Secure Storage and Transmission: Electronic data is protected with encryption and password controls, while physical records are stored in locked, access-controlled environments.

#### Handling Third-Party Data Requests

1. Assess: All requests must be forwarded to the Data Protection Officer (DPO) to assess their validity and relevance.
2. Validate: The DPO will ensure the request complies with legal frameworks. Disclosures will only be made with explicit patient consent or where a legal or ethical justification exists.
3. Document: Every request and its resolution must be documented to ensure traceability and compliance.

#### Confidentiality in Emergency Situations

In critical situations where patient welfare is at risk, staff must balance the duty of confidentiality with the need to provide care. Disclosures without consent should be limited to the minimum necessary information and must be documented with a clear justification. This may include notifying authorities of immediate danger or sharing vital health data with emergency services.


### 3.4 Handling Feedback: Complaints and Compliments

All feedback, whether a complaint or a compliment, is valued as an essential tool for continuous improvement. Livaware is committed to a transparent and fair process for managing feedback, ensuring that individuals feel heard, respected, and supported. This process aligns with our regulatory obligations, including our duty of candour. The full procedural detail is set out in SOP 14 - Concerns, Complaints, and Feedback.

#### Complaint Handling Process (Summary)

- How to Raise a Complaint: Complaints can be made verbally, in writing, or electronically to a staff member or via the central complaints email. A complaints leaflet is available at all service locations and on our website.
- Acknowledge the Complaint: All complaints must be acknowledged within two working days. The acknowledgment should explain the investigation process, provide expected timelines, and name a contact person for updates.
- Investigation and Timeline: A designated investigator will conduct a thorough and impartial review. We aim to complete investigations and provide a full written response within 20 working days.
- Escalation Options: If a complaint cannot be resolved informally or to the complainant's satisfaction, it will be escalated to the Registered Manager. Complainants will also be informed of their right to refer the matter to external bodies, such as the CQC or the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman.

#### Handling Compliments

Compliments are received with gratitude and used to reinforce best practices. They can be shared verbally, in writing, or electronically. Staff should record positive feedback, which will be acknowledged and shared with the relevant individuals and teams to celebrate excellence and inspire continued high performance.

## 4.0 Clinical Operations and Care Delivery

### 4.1 Principles of Clinical Home Care

Delivering clinical care in a home setting presents unique challenges and requires a specialised, flexible approach. The principles and processes outlined in this section ensure that our home care services are delivered with the highest standards of safety, quality, and excellence.

#### Five Core Principles

- Person-Centred Care: Care plans are co-created with patients and families, ensuring their voices and goals guide every decision.
- Holistic and Integrated Approach: We address the physical, emotional, social, and spiritual well-being of each patient, often partnering with specialists.
- Safety First: Rigorous risk assessments of the home environment are conducted. Protocols for infection control, medication, and equipment are meticulously followed.
- Adaptability and Flexibility: Our teams are trained to adapt care delivery to meet the evolving needs of a dynamic home environment without compromising standards.
- Continuous Improvement: We use feedback from patients, families, and staff, alongside proactive audits, to drive improvements in care delivery.

#### The Clinical Home Care Process

- Initial Assessment: A Registered Nurse conducts a comprehensive assessment to identify medical, emotional, and social needs. This includes a thorough risk assessment of the home environment (use Appendix F).
- Care Delivery: Clinical interventions, such as wound care or infusion therapy, are performed according to evidence-based guidelines. Medication is managed safely, and patients and families are educated and empowered to participate in their care.
- Coordination and Communication: Regular multidisciplinary meetings and the use of secure digital platforms ensure cohesive care delivery and real-time updates among the care team, the patient, and external healthcare providers.


### 4.2 Standard Nursing Procedures

All clinical nursing procedures performed by Livaware staff must be conducted safely, competently, and in strict accordance with the Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC) Code of Conduct. To ensure our practice reflects the latest evidence-based guidelines, staff have access to and are expected to use key resources, including the Royal Marsden Manual of Clinical Nursing Procedures.

#### Principles of Clinical Nursing Procedures

- Scope of Practice: Nurses must only perform procedures for which they have received formal training and have been deemed competent. For unfamiliar or advanced procedures, nurses must seek guidance from senior staff or specialists and document the consultation.
- Code of Conduct: As accountable professionals, nurses must prioritise patient safety and dignity, obtain informed consent, and maintain accurate, contemporaneous records for every procedure.
- Home Setting Considerations: Nurses must assess the home environment for risks such as infection control challenges or limited access to emergency equipment. Any adjustments to standard procedures must be documented, and contingency plans must be in place.

#### Phases of a Clinical Procedure

- Pre-Procedure Preparation: Before any procedure, the nurse must confirm alignment with the care plan, review the patient's medical history for allergies or contraindications, verify informed consent has been obtained, and ensure all necessary equipment is available, sterile, and functional.
- Procedure Execution: The nurse must follow the latest evidence-based guidelines, use standard precautions to minimise infection risk, and communicate with the patient throughout to monitor their comfort and address any concerns.
- Post-Procedure Care: After the procedure, the nurse must monitor the patient for adverse reactions, provide clear aftercare instructions to the patient or caregiver, and complete detailed documentation of observations, outcomes, and any challenges encountered.


### 4.3 Medicine Management and Administration

The safe and effective management of medicines is of critical importance at Livaware. Adherence to the procedures in this section is mandatory for all staff involved in the medication process to prevent errors and ensure patient safety, in full compliance with CQC regulations. Detailed procedural standards are in SOP 12.

#### Key Definitions

- Medicinal Product: Any substance administered to a human being for a medicinal purpose, including prescription-only, controlled, pharmacy, and over-the-counter medicines.
- Patient Specific Direction (PSD): A written instruction from a relevant prescriber for a medicine to be supplied or administered to a named patient (e.g., a prescription).
- Medicines Administration Record (MAR): A chart or record that is not a prescription itself but a direction to administer medication. It authorises a qualified individual to administer medication on the prescriber's behalf.

#### Prescription Requirements

All prescriptions must be written legibly in indelible ink and state the following:

- Surname and first forename of the patient, and for outpatients, their address
- Patient's hospital number
- Patient's date of birth
- All allergies (medicinal or other)
- Name of product
- The dose
- The route of administration
- Frequency of administration
- Duration of treatment
- Signature and printed name of the prescriber, along with their GMC Number for an OPD prescription
- Date (including year) of prescription

#### The 7 Rights of Medication Administration

##### Right Patient, Right Medication Chart/Prescription

- Ask the patient to state their full name and date of birth.
- Check that the patient's name and date of birth match the details on the MAR or prescription.
- Always use at least two identifiers.

##### Right Medication

- Check the medicine label and packaging against the prescription.
- Check the patient's known allergies.
- Select the correct form of the drug (e.g., tablet, liquid).
- Check the expiry date on the product.

##### Right Dose

- Check the dose on the MAR or prescription.
- Select the correct strength of the drug preparation.
- If necessary, calculate the dose and have another qualified nurse check the calculation.
- Check the patient's weight if applicable.

##### Right Route

- Check the prescription and confirm the appropriateness of the route.
- Confirm the patient can receive the medication by the prescribed route.
- Use only enteral syringes for administration via enteral feeding tubes (e.g., NG, PEG).

##### Right Time

- Check the prescription for the correct frequency and time of administration.
- Confirm when the last dose was given to avoid duplication.
- Prioritise which drugs should be given first based on their intended effect.

##### Right Reason

- Check the allergy box on the drug chart before administering any medication.
- Confirm the rationale for the medication. Understand why the patient is taking it and that it aligns with their health needs.
- Monitor for the desired effect after administration (e.g., improved blood pressure, reduced pain).

##### Right Documentation

- Sign the drug chart or MAR immediately after administration to record that the dose has been given.
- If a prescribed drug is not given, document the reason clearly.

Any medication-related incident or near miss must be reported immediately using Livaware's incident reporting system (see SOP 12). A healthcare professional involved in such an incident must write a statement and complete a reflective account for their registration revalidation process.


### 4.4 Sharing Care and Treatment with Partners

Collaborative, shared-care arrangements are essential for delivering seamless and comprehensive patient care. By working effectively with external partners such as NHS teams, GPs, and other specialists, we ensure that patients receive cohesive treatment tailored to their individual needs.

#### Roles and Responsibilities in Shared Care

- Livaware Clinical Lead: Serves as the primary point of contact for shared-care arrangements. Oversees the development of care plans and facilitates multidisciplinary team meetings to ensure effective collaboration.
- Partner Healthcare Providers: Collaborate with Livaware to provide consistent care, share relevant patient information securely and in a timely manner, and notify us of any changes to the patient's condition or treatment plan.
- Patients and Families: Are active participants in care planning and decision-making. Encouraged to provide accurate health information and communicate any concerns or changes promptly to the care team.

#### Shared Care Processes

- Communication and Information Sharing: Secure digital platforms, such as encrypted email or patient portals, are used to share information. Patients and families are given clear points of contact. All shared care activities are documented in up-to-date records.
- Care Plan Development: A comprehensive initial assessment incorporates input from all providers. Shared goals and responsibilities are identified, and care plans are regularly reviewed and updated based on feedback from all parties.
- Risk Assessment and Management: A standardised tool is used to evaluate potential risks, such as gaps in communication or medication errors. Contingency plans and clear escalation pathways are established to mitigate these risks.

## 5.0 Safety, Risk, and Incident Management

### 5.1 Integrated Risk Management Framework

Risk management at Livaware is a proactive and continuous process embedded in all our operations. This framework is designed to safeguard patients and staff by systematically identifying, assessing, and mitigating risks. It promotes a culture of awareness and accountability, ensuring that risk management is integrated into strategic planning and daily practice.

#### The Risk Management Process

- Risk Identification: Risks are identified through multiple channels, including regular audits, staff and patient feedback, and incident reports. This includes clinical risks, operational risks (e.g., lone worker safety), and technological risks.
- Risk Assessment: Identified risks are evaluated using a structured matrix that assesses their likelihood and potential impact.
- Risk Mitigation: Actionable mitigation strategies are developed for each identified risk. This could include enhanced training, new safety protocols, or equipment upgrades.
- Monitoring and Review: Mitigation plans are continuously monitored to ensure their effectiveness. Risks are reviewed in monthly team meetings and quarterly governance meetings.

#### Risk Assessment Scoring

Risks are scored by multiplying their Likelihood (rated 1: Rare to 5: Almost Certain) by their Impact (rated 1: Negligible to 5: Catastrophic). The final score dictates the required action:


| Score | Action Required |
| --- | --- |
| 1-5 (Low) | Monitor and review the risk periodically. |
| 6-12 (Moderate) | Mitigation is required. An action plan must be developed. |
| 13-25 (High) | Immediate intervention is required to control the risk. |


### 5.2 Infection Prevention and Control (IPC)

Livaware is committed to maintaining the highest standards of infection prevention and control (IPC) to protect our patients and staff. The practical, mandatory guidance in this section is essential for daily practice and is designed to minimise the risk of infections across all care settings.

#### Standard Precautions

All staff must adhere to the following standard precautions during any patient interaction:

- Hand Hygiene: Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water before and after all patient contact. Use alcohol-based hand sanitiser when soap and water are not available.
- Personal Protective Equipment (PPE): Wear appropriate PPE (gloves, masks, gowns) based on the task and assessed level of risk. Dispose of all PPE safely in designated bins.
- Respiratory Hygiene: Encourage "catch it, bin it, kill it" practices. Provide tissues and waste bins in all patient areas.
- Environmental Cleaning: Regularly clean and disinfect surfaces, equipment, and high-touch areas using approved cleaning agents and methods.
- Safe Waste Disposal: Segregate clinical and non-clinical waste appropriately and follow the established protocols for disposal.

#### Aseptic Non Touch Technique (ANTT)

Livaware uses the Aseptic Non Touch Technique (ANTT) practice framework. ANTT is described in detail in SOP 1 and applies to venepuncture, vascular access device access, dressing changes, and IV medication administration.

#### Outbreak Management

In the event of a potential outbreak, staff must follow a clear procedure:

- Identification: Monitor for and identify any signs of infection clusters among patients or staff.
- Reporting: Immediately notify the Infection Prevention Lead and, for significant outbreaks, the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA).
- Control Measures: Isolate affected individuals where possible and implement enhanced cleaning and disinfection protocols to control the spread.


### 5.3 Waste Management Protocols

Proper waste management is crucial for safety, environmental sustainability, and regulatory compliance. All waste generated through Livaware operations must be handled, segregated, and disposed of according to national standards.

#### Waste Segregation

- Domestic waste (black/clear bag): Non-hazardous, non-clinical waste (e.g., packaging, food waste).
- Offensive/hygiene waste (yellow and black striped bag - "tiger stripe"): Non-infectious clinical waste such as continence pads, sanitary items, gloves, and aprons not contaminated with infectious material.
- Infectious clinical waste (orange bag): Waste contaminated with body fluids that may be infectious - to be sent for alternative treatment.
- Infectious clinical waste with medicinal contamination (yellow bag): Waste contaminated with medicines or pharmaceutically active substances - to be incinerated.
- Sharps (yellow lid sharps bin): Needles, syringes with needles attached, glass ampoules, scalpels. NEVER mix sharps with other waste streams. Do not exceed fill line.
- Cytotoxic/cytostatic waste (purple lid sharps bin): Sharps and waste contaminated with cytotoxic or cytostatic medicines.
- Medicines waste: Unused or partially used medicines to be returned to pharmacy where possible, or disposed of in appropriate waste streams (never general waste).

#### Home Setting Waste Management

In the home setting, clinical waste arrangements should be confirmed with the patient and local authority. Livaware staff must ensure: sharps are disposed of immediately into a designated sharps container at point of use; clinical waste is not left unsecured; and the family/patient understands collection arrangements.


### 5.4 Responding to Accidents and Incidents

A transparent and systematic approach to incident reporting is essential for organisational learning and continuous improvement. The procedure in this section is mandatory for all staff to follow whenever an accident, incident, or near miss occurs. This fosters a culture where staff feel empowered to report issues without fear of blame.

#### Incident Reporting Framework

- Immediate Response: The first priority is to ensure the safety of everyone involved and mitigate any further harm. This includes providing first aid, securing the area, and alerting relevant team members.
- Internal Reporting: All incidents must be reported through Livaware's incident management system within 24 hours. Reports must be factual and objective, avoiding speculative language.
- External Reporting Requirements: Notifiable incidents will be reported to external regulatory bodies, such as the Care Quality Commission (CQC) or the Health and Safety Executive (HSE), within the prescribed timeframes.

#### Incident Categorisation

- No Harm: An event occurred but did not result in any adverse outcome.
- Low Harm: The event resulted in minor injuries or disruptions that did not require extensive intervention.
- Moderate Harm: The event necessitated medical attention or caused significant operational disruption.
- Severe Harm: The event resulted in long-term injury, critical harm, or fatality.


### 5.5 Upholding the Duty of Candour

The Duty of Candour is Livaware's commitment to openness, honesty, and transparency when things go wrong. This legal and ethical obligation ensures that patients and their families are treated with respect and integrity following a "Notifiable Safety Incident."

A Notifiable Safety Incident is defined as an unintended or unexpected incident that has caused or could result in moderate harm, severe harm, or death. Examples include a medication error causing significant short-term harm or a fall resulting in a fracture.

#### Response to a Notifiable Safety Incident

- Initial Response: The first priority is to ensure patient safety and provide any immediate care needed to address the harm. The Registered Manager must be notified as soon as the incident is identified.
- Communication with the Patient: A full, verbal explanation of what occurred must be provided to the patient and/or their family. This must be followed by a written apology, which should be offered within 5 working days for high-severity cases and within 10 working days for less critical incidents. Communication must be adapted to meet any accessibility needs.
- Investigation and Learning: A thorough investigation, often using a 5 Whys analysis to identify the root cause, will be conducted. The findings will be shared with the patient and used to implement system-wide changes to improve services and prevent recurrence.


### 5.6 Safeguarding Procedures (Summary)

Safeguarding is a fundamental duty for every Livaware staff member. We are committed to protecting adults at risk and children/young people from abuse, harm, and neglect. This section provides a summary; the full procedure including escalation routes is in SOP 10.

#### Safeguarding Adults at Risk

An Adult at Risk is a person aged 18 or over who has care and support needs, is experiencing or is at risk of abuse or neglect, and is unable to protect themselves due to those needs.

Staff must be vigilant for potential signs of abuse or neglect, which include:

- Unexplained injuries or changes in behaviour
- Financial irregularities or sudden loss of assets
- Poor hygiene or signs of malnutrition
- Withdrawal, fearfulness, or signs of distress
- Inappropriate or controlling behaviour by carers

#### Reporting Process

1. Raise concerns immediately with the Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL) or their deputy. If there is an immediate danger, contact emergency services first.
2. The DSL will ensure the concern is reported to the local authority safeguarding team within 24 hours.

#### Safeguarding Children and Young People

While Livaware primarily cares for adults, all staff have a responsibility to be vigilant for risks to children and young people (anyone under 18) who may be impacted by our services through family or community connections. The reporting process mirrors the adult procedure - report any concerns immediately to the DSL.

## SOP 1 - Aseptic Non Touch Technique (ANTT) for Invasive Procedures and Device Access

### Purpose

To reduce the risk of healthcare-associated infection by ensuring Key-Parts and Key-Sites remain aseptic during invasive procedures and when accessing indwelling devices, using the ANTT practice framework.


### Scope

Applies to all Livaware staff performing invasive procedures in the home, including venepuncture, IV infusion set-up, access of PICCs and implanted ports, and dressing/connector changes.


### Responsibilities

- Clinician: Selects the appropriate ANTT approach (Standard-ANTT or Surgical-ANTT), prepares the environment, maintains asepsis, and documents the approach used.
- Clinical governance lead: Ensures ANTT training, competency assessment, and audit.


### Key ANTT Concepts

- Key-Parts: Parts of equipment that must remain aseptic (e.g., needle tips, syringe tips, exposed sterile connectors).
- Key-Sites: The patient site that must remain aseptic (e.g., disinfected skin puncture site, vascular access device hub).
- Aseptic fields: A managed workspace that protects Key-Parts/Key-Sites (general or critical aseptic field depending on the procedure).
- Non-touch technique: Avoid touching Key-Parts/Key-Sites directly; if contact would occur, use sterile gloves or sterile forceps as appropriate.


### Selecting Standard-ANTT vs Surgical-ANTT

Select the ANTT approach based on the number of Key-Parts/Key-Sites and the complexity of the procedure.

- Standard-ANTT: For procedures with few Key-Parts/Key-Sites that can be protected by non-touch technique and a general aseptic field (e.g., venepuncture, simple hub access).
- Surgical-ANTT: For procedures with multiple Key-Parts/Key-Sites or higher complexity where a critical aseptic field and sterile gloves are required (e.g., central line dressing changes, complex device access).


### Equipment and Environment

- Alcohol-based hand rub and/or handwashing facilities
- Appropriate PPE (gloves, apron/gown, eye/face protection as required)
- Approved skin antisepsis and hub disinfectant products (compatible with patient and device)
- Aseptic field materials (e.g., sterile drape or tray)
- Sharps container at point of use


### Procedure (Minimum Steps)

1. Assess environment: choose a clean, well-lit area; manage pets/children; prepare a stable surface; minimise interruptions. Use Appendix F (Home Environment Safety Checklist).
2. Perform hand hygiene and don appropriate PPE.
3. Identify and define Key-Parts and Key-Sites for the intended procedure.
4. Prepare and manage an aseptic field suitable for the approach (Standard or Surgical).
5. Perform skin/hub disinfection using an approved agent and allow to dry fully before access.
6. Maintain non-touch technique throughout. If contamination is suspected, stop and replace contaminated items and/or re-disinfect as required.
7. Dispose of sharps immediately at point of use and complete safe waste handling.
8. Complete post-procedure hand hygiene.


### Documentation Requirements

- Procedure performed and indication
- ANTT approach used (Standard or Surgical) and key infection prevention actions (e.g., skin/hub disinfectant used)
- Any breaks in asepsis and corrective actions taken
- Patient response, complications, and aftercare advice


### Escalation and Safety

If sterility/asepsis cannot be maintained in the home environment (for example due to contamination, inability to control the environment, or insufficient equipment), do not proceed. Escalate to the executive nurse lead and agree an alternative plan with the treating team.


### References

- Association for Safe Aseptic Practice (ASAP). ANTT Practice Framework and guidance. https://www.antt.org/
- epic3 national evidence-based guidelines for preventing healthcare-associated infections in NHS hospitals in England (Journal of Hospital Infection / HIS).


## SOP 2 - Venepuncture for Blood Sampling (Adult and Older Child in Home Setting)

### Purpose

To obtain blood samples safely and accurately for laboratory testing, while minimising risk to the patient and clinician and ensuring specimen integrity and traceability.


### Scope

Applies to registered clinicians trained and assessed as competent in venepuncture. Excludes arterial sampling and neonatal/paediatric specialist sampling unless specifically authorised.


### Responsibilities

- Clinician: Confirms test request, patient identity, consent, and sample handling requirements; performs venepuncture using ANTT principles; labels and packages samples correctly; documents and escalates issues.
- Service coordination: Confirms courier/transport arrangements and laboratory acceptance requirements for timing and packaging.


### Contraindications and Cautions

- Do not use an arm with lymphoedema, post-mastectomy side, AV fistula/graft, or local infection/skin breakdown unless explicitly authorised and risk-assessed.
- Use caution with anticoagulated patients, bleeding disorders, frail skin, or difficult access; plan for haemostasis and aftercare.
- If the patient is unwell (e.g., syncope risk, severe anxiety), consider positioning (seated/lying) and support.


### Equipment

- Blood sampling system (vacutainer or needle/syringe as appropriate) and appropriately sized needle/winged set where clinically indicated
- Tourniquet, gloves, skin antisepsis, gauze/cotton wool, tape or dressing, sharps container
- Correct blood collection tubes for requested tests (check expiry)
- Specimen labels and request forms (paper or electronic) with traceable identifiers
- Packaging compliant with laboratory/courier requirements (provided or specified by the laboratory/courier)


### Procedure

1. Confirm test request and any special requirements (fasting, timing, tube type, sample handling, laboratory acceptance criteria). Confirm who is responsible for specimen transport (Livaware does not provide specimen transport unless explicitly commissioned).
2. Identify patient using two identifiers and confirm consent; explain procedure and aftercare.
3. Prepare environment and aseptic field (Standard-ANTT). Perform hand hygiene and don gloves.
4. Select site using clinical judgement. Apply tourniquet briefly, palpate vein, then release tourniquet while preparing equipment if prolonged delay is anticipated.
5. Cleanse skin with approved antiseptic and allow to dry fully.
6. Re-apply tourniquet, anchor vein as appropriate, perform venepuncture, and collect tubes according to local laboratory order of draw. Mix tubes by gentle inversion per manufacturer instructions.
7. Release tourniquet as soon as blood flow is established and before needle withdrawal where appropriate.
8. Withdraw needle, apply pressure until haemostasis achieved, then apply dressing if needed. Advise patient to keep pressure and avoid heavy lifting with that arm for an appropriate period.
9. Dispose of sharps immediately and perform hand hygiene.
10. Label specimens at the point of care in the presence of the patient (or according to local policy). Verify labels match the request.
11. Package samples according to the laboratory/courier requirements and document the handover details (collection time and who the specimen was handed to). Do not transport specimens unless explicitly commissioned and authorised.


### Complications and Management

- Haematoma: Release tourniquet, withdraw needle, apply firm pressure, consider cold compress, document and advise.
- Syncope: Stop procedure, keep patient safe (lying position), monitor, escalate if not recovering promptly.
- Failed attempts: Limit attempts per clinician competence/policy; consider alternative clinician/device; document attempts and plan.
- Needlestick injury: Follow SOP 6 (Sharps Injury and Blood Exposure) immediately.


### Documentation Requirements

- Date/time, indication/tests requested, consent obtained
- Site used, device/needle type and gauge, number of attempts, patient tolerance
- ANTT approach and skin antisepsis used; any variance from SOP and rationale
- Specimen labelling confirmation and handover details (collection time and who the specimen was handed to)
- Any complications and advice given


### References

- World Health Organization. WHO guidelines on drawing blood: best practices in phlebotomy. https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241599221
- Royal College of Nursing. Standards for infusion therapy (includes blood sampling and vascular access care).


## SOP 3 - Peripheral IV Infusion Using Winged Infusion Set (Butterfly) in the Home

### Purpose

To administer short-duration IV therapy via a peripheral vein using a winged infusion set, when clinically appropriate and authorised, while minimising infiltration, phlebitis, and infection risks.


### Scope

Applies to competent registered clinicians. This SOP is for short-duration peripheral infusion only and is not intended for prolonged continuous infusions unless explicitly authorised in the treatment plan.


### Key Safety Position

Winged infusion sets are generally best suited to short, supervised infusions or bolus administration. For therapies requiring sustained access, consider a peripheral cannula, midline, PICC, or implanted port per the treating plan. Do not proceed if the prescribed therapy requires a different access device, or if local policy/manufacturer guidance contraindicates use.


### Equipment

- Winged infusion set with luer lock, extension set as required, and compatible needle-free connector if used
- IV infusion device/flow control as required (e.g., gravity set, pump) per prescription
- Approved skin antisepsis and hub disinfectant products; sterile gauze/dressings; securement materials
- Flush syringes and/or infusion solution as prescribed (do not prepare/compound unless authorised)
- Sharps container, PPE, waste disposal materials


### Procedure

1. Verify prescription/order and monitoring requirements. Confirm allergies and previous infusion reactions. Check emergency plan and escalation route.
2. Assess patient and environment; obtain consent; position patient safely (consider syncope risk).
3. Prepare aseptic field (Standard-ANTT). Hand hygiene and appropriate PPE.
4. Select an appropriate vein and device gauge. Avoid sites near joints when possible to reduce dislodgement risk.
5. Cleanse skin with approved antiseptic; allow to dry fully.
6. Insert winged infusion set using non-touch technique. Confirm placement by aspiration of blood (where clinically appropriate) and/or easy flush without resistance. Do not force flush.
7. Secure device to reduce movement and ensure the connection remains visible for monitoring. Apply an appropriate dressing.
8. Prime and connect the infusion set using aseptic technique. Start infusion at the prescribed rate.
9. Monitor the site frequently for pain, swelling, coolness, leakage, redness, or patient-reported discomfort. Use VIP score (Appendix J) to assess phlebitis. Stop immediately if infiltration/extravasation is suspected.
10. On completion, disconnect and remove the winged set, apply pressure and dressing, and provide aftercare advice.
11. Dispose of sharps immediately and complete hand hygiene.


### Complications and Escalation

- Infiltration/extravasation: Stop infusion, disconnect, follow the medication-specific extravasation guidance if relevant, elevate limb, and escalate to treating team immediately. Document fully.
- Phlebitis: Stop infusion, remove device, manage symptoms, consider alternative access, document and report. Use VIP score (Appendix J).
- Anaphylaxis/acute reaction: Stop infusion, follow SOP 8, call 999 if indicated, and escalate to the treating team.


### Documentation Requirements

- Access type, site, gauge, number of attempts, confirmation of patency, dressing/securement
- Medication/infusion details (name, dose, diluent/volume if applicable, rate, start/stop times)
- Monitoring observations and site checks; VIP score; patient tolerance and any adverse events
- Rationale for using a winged infusion set (if not the default device) and any variance from SOP


### References

- Royal College of Nursing. Standards for infusion therapy.
- Local NHS vascular access guidelines may provide additional device-specific cautions.


## SOP 4 - IV Infusion via Peripherally Inserted Central Catheter (PICC)

### Purpose

To administer IV therapy safely via an existing PICC in a home setting, using aseptic technique and appropriate monitoring, while minimising infection and device-related complications.


### Scope

Applies to clinicians trained and authorised to access and use central vascular access devices. Does not include PICC insertion. Dressing changes and connector changes may require separate competence and may require Surgical-ANTT depending on complexity and local policy. See Appendix I for evidence-based dressing change intervals.


### Pre-Procedure Checks

- Confirm the PICC is intended for use (device type, lumen(s), indication) and that tip position has been verified and documented by the inserting service.
- Inspect insertion site and dressing: intact, clean, dry, and within the expected change interval (see Appendix I). Check for redness, swelling, discharge, pain, or fever/systemic symptoms.
- Check external catheter length against baseline documentation if available; note any change and escalate.
- Confirm prescribed therapy, compatibility, required flushes/locks and monitoring parameters per treatment plan and manufacturer IFU.


### Equipment

- Approved hub/connector disinfectant and supplies for ANTT
- Sterile field materials (tray/drape), appropriate gloves (non-sterile or sterile depending on task)
- Pre-filled flush syringes where available; infusion set/pump as prescribed; extension set/needle-free connector as per device system
- Dressing supplies if required (only if competent and authorised)
- Sharps container and waste disposal materials


### Procedure (Access and Infusion)

1. Verify patient identity, allergies, consent and explain the procedure. Confirm escalation plan.
2. Prepare environment and aseptic field. Perform hand hygiene and don appropriate PPE.
3. Perform a focused assessment of the site/dressing and patient symptoms. If signs of infection, thrombosis, device migration, or unexplained pain, do not proceed and escalate.
4. Disinfect the PICC hub/needle-free connector using an approved method and allow to dry fully.
5. Maintain non-touch technique for Key-Parts. Connect flush syringe using aseptic technique.
6. Assess patency according to local policy (e.g., ability to flush without resistance and/or blood return where required). Do not force flush against resistance; stop and escalate.
7. Connect the infusion set using aseptic technique. Start infusion at the prescribed rate and monitor per plan.
8. During infusion, monitor for pain, swelling of arm/neck/face, leakage, fever, chills, or line alarms; stop infusion and escalate if concerns arise.
9. On completion, disconnect using aseptic technique, apply required flush/lock per prescription/IFU, and ensure clamps are correctly positioned (if applicable).
10. Ensure dressing remains intact. Provide patient aftercare advice and red flags.
11. Dispose of waste safely and perform hand hygiene.


### Complications and Escalation

- Suspected catheter-related infection: Local signs or systemic symptoms: stop therapy if clinically appropriate, escalate urgently to treating team; document observations and actions.
- Occlusion/resistance: Do not force flush; check mechanical causes (clamps, kinks) without breaching asepsis; escalate for further management.
- Suspected thrombosis: Arm swelling/pain, collateral veins: stop infusion, keep line secure, escalate same day/urgent.
- Suspected air embolism: Clamp line, position patient as per emergency guidance, call 999 immediately, and follow SOP 8.


### Documentation Requirements

- Device details (PICC type, lumen used), site assessment findings, external length if recorded, dressing status
- ANTT approach and hub disinfectant used; confirmation of patency and any issues
- Infusion details per prescription and monitoring performed
- Flush/lock performed (type/volume/concentration as per prescription/IFU) and patient education provided
- Any variance from SOP and rationale; complications and escalation actions


### References

- Royal College of Nursing. Standards for infusion therapy (central vascular access devices).
- epic3 guidelines for preventing healthcare-associated infections (aseptic technique and device care).


## SOP 5 - IV Infusion via Implanted Venous Access Port (PORT / TIVAD)

### Purpose

To access an implanted venous port safely using a non-coring needle for IV therapy or blood sampling, maintaining asepsis and minimising infection and mechanical complications.


### Scope

Applies to clinicians trained and authorised for implanted port access. Port insertion/removal is excluded. This SOP assumes use of a non-coring (Huber) needle and appropriate port system components. See Appendix I for care intervals.


### Pre-Procedure Checks

- Confirm port type and indication; review any device card/documentation if available.
- Assess for infection signs (local redness, swelling, warmth, discharge) and systemic symptoms (fever, rigors). Do not access if infection suspected; escalate.
- Confirm prescribed therapy and whether blood return is required before administration (especially for irritant/vesicant medicines).
- Check for allergies to antiseptics/adhesives and plan alternatives if needed.


### Equipment

- Non-coring port access needle of appropriate length and gauge (Huber needle) with extension tubing as required
- Sterile gloves and critical aseptic field materials (Surgical-ANTT)
- Approved skin antisepsis and hub disinfectant; sterile dressing/transparent occlusive dressing
- Flush/lock syringes as prescribed and compatible with device IFU; infusion set/pump as required
- Sharps container and waste disposal materials


### Procedure (Surgical-ANTT Expected)

1. Verify patient identity, consent, allergies and explain the procedure and expected sensations.
2. Prepare environment and a critical aseptic field. Perform hand hygiene. Don apron/gown if required and sterile gloves as per Surgical-ANTT.
3. Palpate and identify the port septum through the skin without contaminating sterile equipment.
4. Disinfect skin over the port with an approved antiseptic using the locally specified technique and allow to dry fully.
5. Prime the non-coring needle/extension set as per local policy to minimise air entry, maintaining asepsis of Key-Parts.
6. Stabilise the port and insert the non-coring needle at 90 degrees through the septum until seated. Secure with an appropriate sterile dressing.
7. Confirm patency according to local policy (commonly includes aspiration for blood return and easy flush without resistance). Do not force flush against resistance; stop and escalate.
8. Connect infusion set using aseptic technique and commence infusion at prescribed rate. Monitor patient and site during therapy.
9. On completion, flush and lock the port as prescribed and per manufacturer IFU. De-access by removing dressing and withdrawing the needle safely; apply pressure if needed and dress.
10. Dispose of sharps immediately and complete hand hygiene.


### Complications and Escalation

- No blood return / resistance / pain: Do not force; consider mechanical checks without breaching asepsis; escalate to treating team for troubleshooting guidance.
- Suspected infection: Escalate urgently; document observations and actions; follow sepsis escalation route where indicated (SOP 8).
- Extravasation risk: If pain, swelling, or leakage occurs during infusion, stop immediately and escalate; follow medication-specific guidance if applicable.


### Documentation Requirements

- Port accessed, needle type/length/gauge, dressing type, confirmation of patency and any troubleshooting
- ANTT approach (Surgical) and antisepsis/hub disinfection products used; any breaks and corrective actions
- Infusion details per prescription (start/stop times, rate, monitoring) and flush/lock performed per plan/IFU
- Patient response, complications, escalation, and aftercare advice


### References

- Royal College of Nursing. Standards for infusion therapy (central vascular access devices).
- Manufacturer IFU for specific port system in use.


## SOP 6 - Sharps Injury and Blood/Body Fluid Exposure (Staff Safety)

### Purpose

To ensure prompt first aid, risk assessment, escalation, and documentation following a sharps injury or blood/body fluid exposure, reducing the risk of transmission of blood-borne viruses and supporting staff wellbeing.


### Scope

Applies to all staff and contractors. This SOP covers percutaneous injury (needlestick/cut) and mucocutaneous exposure (eyes, mouth, broken skin).


### Immediate Actions (First Aid)

1. Stop the procedure safely and make the environment safe (sharps secured, patient supported).
2. For percutaneous injury: encourage bleeding gently (do not suck), wash with soap and running water, and cover with a waterproof dressing.
3. For mucous membrane exposure: rinse thoroughly with water or saline. For eye exposure, irrigate with water or saline.
4. Do not scrub skin aggressively or use harsh chemicals on the wound.


### Escalation and Assessment

1. Inform the Livaware clinical lead immediately as soon as the patient is safe.
2. Arrange urgent occupational health / emergency department assessment according to local access route and time of day, for blood-borne virus risk assessment and advice.
3. Where required, obtain consent and arrangements for source patient testing through appropriate clinical pathways (do not pressure the patient; follow lawful processes).
4. Follow advice regarding post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) and baseline/follow-up testing.


### Documentation and Reporting

- Record the incident promptly: date/time, circumstances, device, injury type, first aid, parties involved, and immediate actions.
- Complete incident reporting in Livaware's system and retain any relevant batch/device information.
- If a RIDDOR report is required, this will be managed through Livaware governance.


### Support

Livaware will provide confidential support to staff following an exposure incident, including clinical follow-up routes and wellbeing support.


### References

- UK Health Security Agency and NHS guidance on management of occupational exposures to blood-borne viruses (follow local pathway).


## SOP 7 - Defensible Documentation for Invasive Procedures and Interventions

### Purpose

To standardise the minimum documentation dataset for invasive procedures and device-related interventions, supporting patient safety, continuity of care, and professional accountability.


### Scope

Applies to all procedures performed under Livaware, including venepuncture, peripheral infusion, PICC/PORT access, and dressing/device care.


### Minimum Dataset - Record Every Time

- Patient identification (two identifiers) and location
- Date/time and clinician name/role (and registration number where applicable)
- Indication for procedure and reference to the treating plan/prescription
- Consent (and capacity considerations where relevant)
- Assessment findings relevant to safety (e.g., site condition, infection signs, allergies)
- Procedure details: technique, site, device type/size, number of attempts, aseptic approach (ANTT Standard/Surgical), antisepsis used
- Outcome and observations: success/failure, patient tolerance, monitoring performed, vitals if relevant
- Complications/adverse events and management; escalation actions and who was contacted
- Aftercare advice provided and patient understanding
- Any variance from SOP or expected pathway and the rationale (clinical judgement)


### Recording Variation in Technique (Practical Approach)

1. State the default expectation (what the SOP would usually require).
2. State what you did instead (clearly and factually).
3. Provide the patient-specific rationale (clinical reasoning, risk/benefit).
4. Describe what you did to mitigate risk (e.g., enhanced monitoring, escalation).
5. Record outcome and any follow-up plan.


### Quality Checks

- Write as if the reader was not present: make it clear enough for another clinician to continue care safely.
- Avoid ambiguous language. Use objective measures where possible.
- Avoid retrospective editing without clear audit trail. Correct errors according to the record system rules.

## SOP 8 - Recognition and Management of Acute Deterioration and Medical Emergencies in the Home

### Purpose

To ensure early recognition of acute deterioration, prompt escalation to the appropriate emergency or medical route, and safe immediate actions while awaiting help. Livaware is not an emergency response service; this SOP is about recognition, first response within competence, and escalation.


### Scope

Applies to all Livaware staff providing in-home clinical care, particularly during and after invasive procedures and infusion therapies. Applies primarily to adults. Paediatric emergencies must follow paediatric-specific guidance and escalation routes.


### Responsibilities

- Clinician: Recognise deterioration, undertake immediate safety actions within competence, call 999/111/GP/consultant as appropriate, and document clearly.
- On-call senior/executive nurse: Provide clinical support, coordinate escalation and communication, and ensure incident reporting and follow-up.
- Service coordination: Ensure escalation plans, key contacts, and location/access details are available; support ambulance access if required.
- Clinical governance lead: Ensure staff maintain up-to-date BLS/ILS training, anaphylaxis training, and periodic drills/audits.


### Equipment (Minimum Expectation)

- Working phone with ability to call 999; exact address and access instructions available
- Observation equipment as commissioned for the visit (e.g., BP, pulse, RR, SpO2, temperature; blood glucose where indicated)
- Access to the patient's current medication list, allergies, diagnosis summary, and escalation/treatment escalation plan (including DNACPR/ReSPECT where applicable)
- If commissioned and authorised: oxygen, suction, AED, and/or emergency medicines under a PSD/PGD and within competence


### Procedure

#### 1. Before Starting an Invasive Procedure or Infusion

- Check whether there is an agreed treatment escalation plan (including DNACPR/ReSPECT). Clarify where documents are kept in the home.
- Confirm who to contact (treating consultant/CNS/GP) and the Livaware on-call route.
- Confirm location details for emergency services (exact address, entry instructions, lift codes, parking constraints).

#### 2. Recognise and Assess Deterioration

- If the patient looks acutely unwell or reports new/worsening symptoms, pause non-essential tasks and assess ABCDE (Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure).
- Record a full set of vital signs where feasible and safe. For adults, calculate NEWS2 if trained and the tool is available, but do not rely on NEWS2 alone; consider baseline and trajectory.
- If the patient deteriorates during an infusion or immediately after a drug/flush, stop the infusion and treat as a potential adverse reaction until proven otherwise.

#### 3. Call 999 Immediately (Do Not Delay) if ANY of the Following Apply:

- Unresponsive, collapsed, or not breathing normally (suspected cardiac arrest)
- Airway compromise: swelling, stridor, unable to speak in sentences, or rapidly worsening voice/airway symptoms
- Severe breathing difficulty: marked increased work of breathing, cyanosis, or SpO2 <94% with acute illness (or below the patient's agreed baseline range where documented)
- Circulatory compromise/shock: systolic BP <90 mmHg with symptoms, severe tachycardia with collapse, or signs of poor perfusion (cold/clammy, confusion)
- Suspected stroke (FAST positive) or sudden new focal neurological deficit
- Persistent seizure activity, recurrent seizures, or post-ictal state not recovering as expected
- Severe chest pain suggestive of acute coronary syndrome, or chest pain with collapse/arrhythmia symptoms
- Life-threatening allergic reaction/anaphylaxis (see anaphylaxis section below)
- Major haemorrhage or uncontrolled bleeding
- NEWS2 ≥7, OR clinician concern with rapidly rising score, OR any single parameter score of 3 with acute change

#### Urgent Medical Review (Non-999)

If 999 is not indicated but urgent same-day medical review is required, use the agreed escalation route in the care plan (treating consultant/CNS/GP) and/or NHS 111 as appropriate. Inform the Livaware on-call senior nurse. If the patient worsens, switch to 999.

#### While Awaiting Help

- Maintain patient safety: position appropriately (e.g., lie flat with legs elevated for shock unless breathing is compromised; recovery position if unconscious and breathing).
- Continue ABCDE reassessment. Repeat and record observations at a frequency proportionate to acuity.
- Stop infusions/flushes if reaction suspected; keep vascular access in situ unless it is unsafe to do so or specifically directed by emergency services.
- Prepare key information for responders: allergies, medication list, diagnosis, recent interventions/infusions, and baseline observations.
- Arrange for someone to meet the ambulance and direct them in (if possible).

#### Cardiac Arrest / Unresponsive Patient (Adult BLS)

If the person is unresponsive: call 999 immediately (use speakerphone if possible) and obtain an AED if available. Assess breathing. If breathing is abnormal or absent, start CPR immediately and follow AED prompts. Continue until help arrives, the person shows signs of life, or you are exhausted.

If a valid DNACPR/ReSPECT decision applies, follow it. If you are uncertain about validity/applicability, start BLS and call 999, then clarify as soon as practicable.

#### Anaphylaxis (Emergency)

Treat as anaphylaxis if there is a sudden onset of airway and/or breathing and/or circulation problems, usually with skin/mucosal changes, particularly after drug exposure (e.g., infusion, flush, dressing product).

##### Immediate Actions

- Stop the trigger (e.g., stop infusion). Call 999 and state "suspected anaphylaxis".
- Position: lie flat with legs elevated; sitting may help breathing. If pregnant, lie on left side.
- Apply monitoring if available (SpO2, pulse, BP).
- Administer IM adrenaline ONLY if authorised (PSD/PGD) and within competence, using 1 mg/mL (1:1000) concentration into the anterolateral thigh.

##### IM Adrenaline Dosing

- Adult and child >12 years: 500 micrograms IM (0.5 mL)
- Child 6–12 years: 300 micrograms IM (0.3 mL)
- Child 6 months–6 years: 150 micrograms IM (0.15 mL)
- Child <6 months: 100–150 micrograms IM (0.1–0.15 mL)

If no response, repeat IM adrenaline after 5 minutes while awaiting emergency services. Prepare for CPR if deterioration continues. Intravenous adrenaline for anaphylaxis must only be given by experienced specialists in an appropriate setting.


### Documentation Requirements

- Presenting concern and context (including infusion/medicine exposure, time of onset)
- Observations (and NEWS2 score if used), times recorded, and trend
- Escalation actions: time 999/111/clinician called, advice received, who attended, and handover given
- Immediate actions taken and patient response
- Any medicines administered (name, dose, route, time, batch if available) and authorisation basis (PSD/PGD)


### Post-Event Actions

Complete Livaware incident reporting (including near-miss). Consider duty of candour requirements. Ensure follow-up plan is recorded and communicated to the treating team and family as appropriate.


### References

- Resuscitation Council UK. Adult basic life support guidelines (2025).
- Resuscitation Council UK. Emergency treatment of anaphylaxis: Guidelines for healthcare providers (May 2021).
- Royal College of Physicians. National Early Warning Score (NEWS) 2 (updated December 2017).


## SOP 9 - Cold Chain and Temperature Management for Medicines (Home Setting)

### Purpose

To maintain the safety and effectiveness of medicines that require temperature control (e.g., 2–8°C refrigerated medicines), and to provide a defensible approach to monitoring, documentation, and management of temperature excursions.


### Scope

Applies to Livaware staff when receiving, transporting (where commissioned), storing, preparing, or administering medicines that have temperature storage requirements. Does not cover laboratory specimen transport (out of scope unless explicitly commissioned).


### Definitions

- Cold chain medicine: A medicine that must be stored between 2°C and 8°C unless otherwise specified by manufacturer.
- Temperature excursion: Any recorded or suspected period outside the required temperature range or storage condition (including unknown storage history).
- Quarantine: Segregation of a medicine pending suitability advice; do not administer.


### Responsibilities

- Clinician: Confirm storage requirements, monitor and record temperatures where within scope, and quarantine/escalate if excursions occur.
- Service coordination: Confirm pharmacy supply arrangements, transport responsibilities, and availability of appropriate storage at the patient's home.
- Clinical governance lead: Ensure equipment is appropriate (e.g., validated cool bags/thermometers), training is delivered, and audits occur.


### Equipment

- Validated cool bag or cool box (as per supplier/manufacturer guidance) and conditioned ice packs/gel packs
- Min/max thermometer or data logger for transport and/or storage where within scope
- Temperature log (Appendix G)
- Clear quarantine label/container (e.g., "Do not use - temperature excursion")


### Procedure

#### 1. Confirm Storage Requirements Before Handling

- Check prescription/label and manufacturer information (e.g., packaging/PIL/eMC) for required temperature range and permitted time out of refrigeration.
- If requirements are unclear or conflicting, do not administer and escalate to the supplying pharmacy/manufacturer information and the treating clinician.

#### 2. Receipt of Medicines

- Check medicine name/strength/form, expiry date, batch/lot (where available), packaging integrity, and that the item is labelled as requiring refrigeration if applicable.
- Where a temperature indicator/data logger is supplied, check and record the reading/status.
- If the storage history is unknown, or packaging suggests a possible excursion, quarantine and escalate.

#### 3. Transport (ONLY Where Explicitly Commissioned and Authorised)

- Keep medicines in original packaging, out of direct sunlight, and minimise journey time.
- Place medicines in the cool bag/box with conditioned packs to maintain 2–8°C. Avoid direct contact between ice packs and medicines (use a barrier) to reduce freezing risk.
- Use a thermometer/data logger where required. Record departure time, arrival time, and temperatures (Appendix G).

#### 4. Storage at the Patient's Home

- Prefer a dedicated medicines refrigerator (pharmaceutical grade where commissioned). If using a domestic fridge, store medicines away from the door and freezer compartment, allow airflow, and keep separated from food where practicable.
- If Livaware is responsible for ongoing storage monitoring, record fridge temperatures at the defined frequency (minimum daily on days of use) using a calibrated thermometer and document in Appendix G.

#### 5. Preparation for Administration

- Remove medicines from refrigeration immediately before preparation unless manufacturer instructions require a specific warm-up period. Record time out of fridge where relevant.
- Do not leave temperature-sensitive medicines at room temperature longer than permitted by manufacturer guidance.

#### 6. Managing Temperature Excursions (2–8°C medicines)

- Quarantine the affected medicine and label clearly "Do not use". Do not administer until suitability is confirmed.
- Take immediate remedial action to restore correct storage conditions (e.g., check fridge door closure, power supply).
- Escalate to the supplying pharmacy/manufacturer information (or NHS Specialist Pharmacy Service where applicable) for stability advice, and inform the treating clinician. Document advice and outcome.
- Complete incident reporting if a medicine was administered after an excursion, or if patient harm/potential harm occurred.


### Documentation Requirements

- Storage requirements confirmed and source checked (label/IFU)
- Transport/storage temperatures and times where within scope (Appendix G)
- Batch/lot number and expiry date where relevant
- Any temperature excursions, quarantine actions, advice sought, and final disposition (used/returned/discarded)


### References

- NHS Specialist Pharmacy Service (SPS). Managing temperature excursions (last updated July 2024).
- Care Quality Commission (CQC). Storing medicines in fridges and cold chain expectations (updated November 2025).


## SOP 10 - Safeguarding Adults and Children (Policy Summary and Escalation Routes)

### Purpose

To ensure all Livaware staff recognise safeguarding concerns, respond appropriately, and escalate promptly to protect adults and children from abuse or neglect.


### Scope

Applies to all Livaware staff (clinical and non-clinical) who may identify safeguarding concerns in the course of providing care or coordination. This is an abbreviated policy summary; local authority contact details must be maintained in the Livaware contact directory.


### Key Principles

- Safeguarding is everyone's responsibility.
- If you suspect immediate danger or a crime in progress, call 999 first.
- Do not investigate. Preserve evidence and document objectively.
- Respect the person's wishes where possible, but share information without consent where necessary to prevent serious harm or protect a child.


### Responsibilities

- All staff: Remain alert to signs of abuse/neglect, respond, escalate, and document.
- Safeguarding Lead (Adults and Children): [Name / Role / Contact] - provides advice, receives internal reports, coordinates referrals and follow-up.
- Deputy Safeguarding Lead: [Name / Role / Contact].


### Signs and Types of Abuse/Neglect (Examples)

- Physical: Unexplained injuries, frequent falls, signs of restraint, delayed presentation.
- Psychological/emotional: Fearfulness, intimidation, humiliation, controlling behaviour.
- Sexual: Disclosure, genital injury, inappropriate contact, exploitation.
- Neglect/acts of omission: Poor hygiene, malnutrition, pressure damage, medication withheld, unsafe environment.
- Financial/material: Unpaid bills despite resources, coercion, missing money, unusual transactions.
- Domestic abuse/coercive control: Partner/family monitoring, isolation, fear, restricted access to phone/appointments.
- Modern slavery/human trafficking: Restricted freedom, fear, poor living conditions, withheld documents.


### Procedure

#### 1. Immediate Safety

- If there is immediate risk of harm or a medical emergency, call 999 and follow SOP 8 as needed.
- If a crime is suspected but there is no immediate danger, consider police 101 (or 999 if urgent).

#### 2. Respond to Disclosure (If the Person Tells You About Abuse)

- Listen calmly, take the concern seriously, and avoid leading questions.
- Explain limits of confidentiality: you may need to share information to keep them or others safe.
- Do not promise to keep secrets or to take specific outcomes.

#### 3. Escalate Internally Immediately

Contact the Safeguarding Lead (or deputy) the same day. If unavailable and risk is significant, escalate to statutory services directly and inform Livaware as soon as practicable.

#### 4. Refer to Statutory Services

- Adults: Refer to the local authority Adult Safeguarding team (Care Act safeguarding enquiry route) as per the Livaware contact directory.
- Children: Refer to local authority Children's Social Care / MASH as per the Livaware contact directory. If a child is at immediate risk, call 999.

#### 5. Documentation

- Record facts: what you saw/heard, the person's exact words (verbatim where possible), dates/times, who was present.
- Record any injuries using a body map if applicable (do not photograph unless explicitly authorised by policy and consent/legal basis).
- Record actions taken, who you contacted, and advice given/received.

#### 6. Follow-Up

Continue to support the person within Livaware's scope. Avoid confronting alleged perpetrators. Ensure staff safety planning (e.g., two-person visit) if indicated.


### National Helplines (Supplementary)

- Emergency services: 999. Non-emergency police: 101.
- NSPCC helpline (children): 0808 800 5000. Childline: 0800 1111.
- National Domestic Abuse Helpline: 0808 2000 247.


### Documentation Requirements

- What raised the concern (facts only) and any disclosure (verbatim where possible)
- Capacity/consent considerations and information-sharing rationale (if shared without consent)
- Internal escalation route used (Safeguarding Lead contacted) and statutory referrals made


### References

- Care Act 2014 statutory guidance (adult safeguarding).
- Working Together to Safeguard Children (statutory guidance).


## SOP 11 - Consent, Capacity, and Best-Interests Decision-Making (Mental Capacity Act Aligned)

### Purpose

To ensure that consent for care and procedures is valid, that capacity is assessed and documented when in doubt, and that decisions for people who lack capacity are made and recorded in a lawful and defensible way.


### Scope

Applies to all clinical interactions and particularly to invasive/high-risk procedures (e.g., venepuncture, vascular access device access, IV infusions).


### Definitions

- Valid consent: Voluntary, informed, and given by a person with capacity for the specific decision at the time.
- Capacity: Decision-specific and time-specific; a person may have capacity for some decisions and not others.
- Best interests: The decision-making framework used when a person lacks capacity for a specific decision (Mental Capacity Act 2005).


### Responsibilities

- Clinician: Obtain and document consent; assess capacity where indicated; escalate complex cases to the on-call senior nurse and the treating clinician.
- On-call senior/executive nurse: Support complex capacity/best-interests situations and coordinate communication with family, LPA, and treating teams.


### Procedure - Obtaining and Documenting Consent

#### 1. Confirm the Decision Required

- Explain what will be done, why, and the material risks/benefits and alternatives (including the option to decline).
- Use language the patient can understand; use interpreters where needed; allow time for questions.

#### 2. Confirm Voluntariness and Ability to Consent

Check for coercion or undue influence (particularly in home settings). If suspected, pause and escalate (see SOP 10 Safeguarding).

#### 3. Document Consent

- For routine, low-risk care, contemporaneous note of verbal consent is usually sufficient.
- For invasive/high-risk procedures, document explicit verbal consent and use Appendix E (Consent and Capacity Assessment Template) where appropriate.


### Procedure - When Capacity Is in Doubt

Apply the Mental Capacity Act principles: presume capacity unless there is reason to doubt; support decision-making; allow unwise decisions; act in best interests if capacity is lacking; choose the least restrictive option.

#### Two-Stage Test (Decision-Specific)

- Stage 1: Is there an impairment of, or disturbance in, the functioning of the mind or brain? (temporary or permanent).
- Stage 2: If yes, does this impairment mean the person is unable to make the specific decision now? Assess ability to understand, retain, use/weigh, and communicate the decision.

#### If the Person Lacks Capacity for the Decision

- Pause non-urgent procedures and escalate to the treating clinician and Livaware senior nurse.
- Check for a valid and applicable Lasting Power of Attorney (Health and Welfare) or court-appointed deputy, and involve them where appropriate.
- Make a best-interests decision, involving the person as much as possible, and consulting those close to them where appropriate.
- Document the process clearly (use Appendix E).

#### Emergencies

If urgent treatment is required to prevent serious deterioration and consent cannot be obtained (e.g., the person is unconscious), act in the person's best interests and escalate via SOP 8. Document rationale and actions fully.


### Documentation Requirements

- What information was provided (including material risks/alternatives), questions asked, and the patient's decision
- If capacity assessed: evidence for stages 1 and 2, and outcome
- If best interests decision: who was consulted, options considered, rationale, and least restrictive approach


### References

- Mental Capacity Act 2005 and associated Code of Practice.


## SOP 12 - Medicines Administration, Infusion Safety Checks, and Medication Incident Reporting

### Purpose

To standardise minimum safety checks for medicines administration (including IV therapies), and to ensure medication errors and near-misses are managed, escalated, and reported in a defensible way.


### Scope

Applies to all Livaware staff involved in medicines administration, preparation, checking, and documentation. Includes IV infusions via peripheral access, PICC, and PORT. Does not authorise prescribing.


### Responsibilities

- Clinician: Perform required checks, administer within competence and authorisation, monitor and document, and report incidents/near-misses.
- On-call senior/executive nurse: Support clinical decision-making, coordinate escalation to prescribers/pharmacy, and ensure incident learning.


### Procedure - Pre-Administration Safety Checks (Minimum)

#### 1. Confirm Legal/Clinical Authority

- Confirm there is an appropriate prescription/order or written plan from the treating clinician and that it matches the product supplied.
- Confirm your competence/authorisation for the route and medicine (including any PGD/PSD requirement).

#### 2. Apply a Structured Check: The "5 Rights" Plus Documentation

- Right patient: Two identifiers and allergy status.
- Right medicine: Name, formulation, and indication match the order.
- Right dose: Calculate/check dose and concentration; confirm dilution where relevant.
- Right route: Confirm route/device access is appropriate (e.g., central vs peripheral compatibility).
- Right time: Correct schedule, rate, and duration; check last dose/time and any required monitoring timings.
- Right documentation: Chart/record contemporaneously with batch/expiry where appropriate.

#### 3. Product Integrity

Check expiry date, batch/lot (where available), appearance, container integrity, and storage history (including cold chain - see SOP 9).

#### 4. High-Risk Medicines and Infusions

- Use an independent double-check where feasible for high-risk medicines (e.g., insulin, anticoagulants, opioids, concentrated electrolytes) and for dose calculations.
- For IV infusions: confirm line patency, compatibility, pump settings/rate, and monitoring plan; ensure escalation plan is in place (SOP 8).


### Procedure - Controlled Drugs (CDs) in the Home (Principles)

Controlled drugs may be present for symptom control (e.g., palliative care). Livaware staff may administer CDs only when prescribed and within competence. Livaware does not supply stock medicines.

Minimum expectations:

- Confirm the prescription and check identity/allergies as above.
- Store CDs securely in line with the patient's arrangements and local policy; if there is a concern about security/diversion, escalate (SOP 10).
- Record administration clearly (dose, route, time, effect) and reconcile remaining quantities where you are responsible for handling/stock checks.
- If a second checker/witness is required by the care plan or local policy, document names and roles.


### Medication Errors and Near-Misses

A medication error is any preventable event that may cause or lead to inappropriate medication use or patient harm. A near-miss is an error intercepted before reaching the patient.

#### Immediate Actions (Always)

- Make the patient safe: assess ABCDE as needed, take observations, and provide immediate care within competence (SOP 8).
- If an error has occurred or is suspected, do not conceal it. Inform the patient (and family where appropriate) in line with candour requirements and the situation.
- Contact the treating clinician/prescriber urgently for clinical advice (e.g., monitoring, reversal, omission of next dose).
- Inform the Livaware on-call senior nurse as soon as practicable.
- Preserve evidence: retain packaging/syringes/infusion bag where safe; note batch/expiry.

#### Reporting and Documentation

- Document factual timeline: what was intended, what happened, when it was identified, actions taken, and patient outcome.
- Complete Livaware incident reporting (error or near-miss).
- Where applicable, support external reporting pathways as directed by governance (e.g., to the supplying pharmacy or manufacturer).


### Documentation Requirements

- Prescription/order details, indication, consent, and checks performed
- Medicine details: name, dose, route, time, dilution/infusion rate, batch/expiry where applicable
- Monitoring performed and patient response/observations
- Any variance from plan/SOP and rationale


### References

- NMC Code (medicines management and record keeping responsibilities).


## SOP 13 - Clinical Handover and Inter-Professional Communication (SBAR)

### Purpose

To ensure safe continuity of care through structured handover within Livaware and clear communication with external professionals (consultants, CNS teams, GPs, community services).


### Scope

Applies to all clinical handovers (shift-to-shift, clinician-to-clinician, and escalation communications) and to communications that change the care plan or document clinical advice.


### Responsibilities

- Clinician handing over: Provide accurate, structured, and timely information and document key communications.
- Receiving clinician: Confirm understanding, clarify uncertainties, and review the record before delivering care.
- Named senior/executive nurse: Oversee care coordination, ensure escalation routes are activated, and maintain a single coherent plan across providers.


### When to Hand Over (Minimum Triggers)

- At every shift change or transfer of responsibility
- After any acute deterioration, escalation call, ED attendance, or hospital admission/discharge
- After invasive procedures where follow-up is required (e.g., line complications, abnormal observations, suspected infection)
- When there is any change to the treating clinician's plan, prescription, or monitoring requirements


### Handover Format (SBAR)

Use SBAR for verbal and written handover. Minimum content:

- Situation: Who the patient is, current problem, and immediate concerns.
- Background: Key diagnoses, current treatments, escalation plan/DNACPR/ReSPECT status, allergies, relevant history.
- Assessment: Observations/NEWS2 (if used), response to treatment, line/device status, pain/symptom status, risks identified.
- Recommendation: What needs to happen next, time-critical actions, monitoring frequency, and escalation thresholds.


### Communication with External Professionals

#### 1. Prepare Before Contacting the Treating Team

- Have the record open. Summarise the issue using SBAR, including objective data (observations, timings, device details).
- State clearly what you are requesting (e.g., medication adjustment request, review, advice on symptom management, VAD concern).

#### 2. Document All Clinical Advice

- Record who you spoke to (name, role, organisation), date/time, key advice given, and agreed actions.
- If advice changes the care plan, ensure it is reflected in the care plan and communicated to relevant staff.

#### 3. Escalation If No Response

Follow the patient's escalation plan and time-criticality. If you cannot reach the intended clinician and the issue is urgent, use alternative routes (out-of-hours GP/111/999) and inform the Livaware senior nurse.


### Documentation Requirements

- Time and method of handover (verbal/phone/written) and who handed over to whom
- SBAR summary and any outstanding actions/risks
- External communications: who was contacted, advice received, and impact on care plan


## SOP 14 - Concerns, Complaints, and Feedback

### Purpose

To ensure that concerns, complaints, and feedback from patients, families, and stakeholders are welcomed, handled fairly and promptly, and used to improve care quality. This SOP supports Livaware's commitment to openness, learning, and person-centred care.


### Scope

Applies to all Livaware staff (clinical and non-clinical). Covers verbal and written concerns/complaints from patients, families, representatives, and external professionals. Does not cover safeguarding concerns (escalate via SOP 10) or clinical emergencies (escalate via SOP 8).


### Responsibilities

- All staff: Listen, acknowledge, attempt early resolution where appropriate, escalate as needed, and document factually.
- Complaints Lead: [Name / Role / Contact] - coordinates investigation, ensures timely responses, liaises with complainants, and identifies learning themes.
- Named Registered Manager / Executive Nurse: Oversees serious complaints, ensures regulatory reporting where required, and ensures action plans are implemented.
- Clinical governance lead: Reviews complaints for themes, integrates learning into training/policy updates, and reports to board/governance meetings.


### Definitions

- Concern: An informal expression of dissatisfaction or worry, often resolved through explanation or immediate action.
- Complaint: A formal expression of dissatisfaction requiring investigation and a written response.
- Compliment/positive feedback: Recognition of good practice; should be shared with staff and used to reinforce quality.


### Principles

1. We welcome feedback and treat all concerns and complaints as opportunities to improve.
2. We respond promptly, fairly, and with empathy.
3. We separate the complaint from ongoing care: the patient continues to receive high-quality care throughout the process.
4. We maintain confidentiality and handle personal data in line with UK GDPR.
5. We support staff involved in complaints with a fair, non-punitive approach focused on learning.


### Procedure

#### 1. Receiving a Concern or Complaint

- Listen carefully and empathetically. Do not become defensive or make promises about specific outcomes.
- Acknowledge the concern and thank the person for raising it.
- If the person is distressed or vulnerable, ensure they are safe and supported (involve family/advocate if appropriate and with consent).
- If the concern relates to immediate safety or safeguarding, escalate via SOP 8 or SOP 10 immediately and inform the person of the action taken.

#### 2. Early Resolution (Concerns)

- Many concerns can be resolved immediately through explanation, apology where appropriate, or a small corrective action (e.g., clarifying information, addressing a scheduling issue).
- If you can resolve the concern on the spot and the person is satisfied, document the concern and resolution in the clinical record and inform your line manager/executive nurse the same day.
- If the concern cannot be resolved immediately or the person requests a formal response, treat it as a complaint and proceed to step 3.

#### 3. Escalating to Formal Complaint

- Inform the Complaints Lead within 24 hours (or next working day).
- Provide the Complaints Lead with a factual summary: who raised the concern, what the concern is, when it occurred, and any immediate actions taken.

The Complaints Lead will contact the complainant within 3 working days to:

- Acknowledge receipt of the complaint in writing (letter or email as appropriate)
- Confirm understanding of the issues raised
- Outline the investigation process and expected timeframe for response
- Provide the Complaints Lead contact details

#### 4. Investigation

- The Complaints Lead (with support from the Named Registered Manager or relevant clinical lead) will gather factual information: review records, speak to staff involved, and consult with external professionals where relevant.
- Identify what happened, why it happened, and whether care met expected standards.
- Consider whether duty of candour applies (if a notifiable safety incident occurred).
- Staff involved in the complaint will be given the opportunity to provide a written statement and will be supported throughout (non-punitive approach unless gross misconduct is identified).
- Investigation timeline: aim for completion within 20 working days for straightforward complaints; complex cases may require longer (keep complainant informed of progress).

#### 5. Response

The response will be in writing (letter or email) and will include:

- A summary of the complaint as understood
- Factual findings from the investigation
- An explanation of what happened and why (without jargon)
- An apology where care fell short of expected standards (in line with duty of candour)
- Actions taken or planned to prevent recurrence
- Information about further options if the complainant remains dissatisfied (see step 6)

The response is signed by the Named Registered Manager or Medical Director (depending on the nature of the complaint). A copy of the response and all investigation records are stored securely in the complaints file and logged in the complaints register.

#### 6. Further Escalation (If Complainant Remains Dissatisfied)

If the complainant is not satisfied with Livaware's response, they have the right to escalate to:

- Care Quality Commission (CQC): For concerns about CQC-registered services. National Customer Service Centre: 03000 616161. Email: enquiries@cqc.org.uk. Online: https://www.cqc.org.uk/give-feedback-on-care
- Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman (PHSO): For complaints about NHS-funded care or where NHS services are involved. PHSO: 0345 015 4033. Online: https://www.ombudsman.org.uk/
- Professional regulators (e.g., NMC for nursing concerns): Where professional conduct is in question.

## Appendix H - Quality Monitoring and Clinical Audit Framework

### Purpose

This appendix sets out Livaware's minimum quality monitoring and clinical audit activities to ensure continuous improvement, regulatory compliance, and patient safety.


### Principles

- Audit is about learning and improvement, not blame.
- Findings are shared transparently with staff and used to update training and SOPs.
- Audit schedule is reviewed annually and adjusted based on risk, incident trends, and regulatory expectations.


### Governance Oversight

- Clinical Governance Lead: Coordinates audit schedule, ensures completion, presents findings to governance meetings.
- Named Registered Manager / Executive Nurse: Ensures action plans are implemented and monitored.
- Medical Director: Provides clinical oversight and sign-off on clinical audit findings where appropriate.


### Minimum Annual Audit Schedule

#### 1. ANTT Compliance Audit (Quarterly)

Objective: Ensure aseptic technique standards are maintained in practice.

Method: Observe or retrospectively review a sample of 10 procedures per quarter (venepuncture, PICC/PORT access, peripheral infusion). Use checklist: hand hygiene, PPE, skin/hub disinfection and drying time, non-touch technique, sharps disposal, documentation. Sample across different clinicians and settings.

Standard: 95% compliance with all critical steps.

Action: Targeted retraining, competency reassessment, review of equipment/resources.

#### 2. Vascular Access Device (VAD) Complication Review (Quarterly)

Objective: Monitor device-related infection, occlusion, and phlebitis rates.

Method: Review all PICC/PORT/peripheral access episodes in the quarter. Identify: infections (local or systemic), occlusions requiring intervention, phlebitis (any grade), infiltration/extravasation events. Calculate rate per 1000 device days.

Standard: Benchmark against national data (e.g., <2 infections per 1000 central line days per epic3/RCN guidance).

Action: Case review, technique audit, product/equipment review.

#### 3. Medication Safety and Error Review (Quarterly)

Objective: Identify medication errors, near-misses, and high-risk medicine handling.

Method: Review all medication incident reports (errors and near-misses). Categorise by type. Review documentation of high-risk medicine double-checks (sample 10 per quarter).

Standard: Zero harm from medication errors; 100% reporting of near-misses; 95% compliance with double-check for high-risk medicines.

Action: Root cause analysis for harm events, refresher training, SOP updates.

#### 4. NEWS2 and Escalation Appropriateness Audit (6-monthly)

Objective: Ensure deterioration is recognised and escalated appropriately.

Method: Review cases where NEWS2 ≥5 or where emergency services were called. Check escalation timing, SBAR use, documented outcome. Sample: all emergency escalations + 10 random elevated NEWS2 cases.

Standard: 100% of NEWS2 ≥7 escalated appropriately; SBAR documented in 95% of escalation communications.

Action: Review escalation pathways, reinforce training, update SOP 8 if gaps identified.

#### 5. Cold Chain Compliance Audit (6-monthly)

Objective: Ensure temperature-sensitive medicines are handled and documented correctly.

Method: Review temperature logs (Appendix G) for all refrigerated medicines in the period. Check daily recording, excursion management, documentation.

Standard: 100% of temperature logs completed; 100% of excursions quarantined and escalated.

Action: Equipment checks, staff retraining, supplier review.

#### 6. Consent and Capacity Documentation Audit (Annual)

Objective: Ensure MCA-compliant consent processes are followed.

Method: Review a sample of 20 invasive procedures across the year. Check consent documentation, capacity assessments where applicable, best interests rationale where applicable.

Standard: 100% consent documented; 100% capacity assessments defensible where applicable.

Action: Staff training on MCA, template updates, clinical supervision.

#### 7. Safeguarding Documentation and Escalation Audit (Annual)

Objective: Ensure safeguarding concerns are recognised and escalated appropriately.

Method: Review all safeguarding concerns/referrals in the year. Check factual documentation, timely escalation to Safeguarding Lead, statutory referral where indicated.

Standard: 100% documented; 100% escalated appropriately.

Action: Safeguarding supervision, training updates.

#### 8. Clinical Handover and Communication Audit (Annual)

Objective: Ensure SBAR framework is used and clinical communications are documented.

Method: Review a sample of 20 shift handovers and 10 external clinical communications. Check SBAR structure, documentation completeness, follow-up actions.

Standard: 90% compliance with SBAR structure; 100% of clinical advice documented.

Action: Handover training, template provision, supervision.

#### 9. Record Keeping Audit (Annual)

Method: Review a random sample of 30 clinical records across the year. Check against NMC standards: contemporaneous, accurate, attributable, legible, factual, complete (assessment-plan-action-outcome).

Standard: 95% compliance with all criteria.

Action: Individual feedback, group training, documentation template updates.

Method: Review all complaints, concerns, and compliments in the year. Identify themes (positive and negative). Consider patient/family surveys.

Standard: 100% complaints acknowledged within 3 working days; learning actions identified for all upheld complaints.

## Appendix I - Vascular Access Device Care Intervals and Dressing Change Schedule

### Purpose

To provide evidence-based intervals for routine care of vascular access devices (VADs) to minimise infection and mechanical complications while maintaining device function.


### Scope

Applies to PICCs, implanted ports, and peripheral IV access in Livaware's scope. Does not replace manufacturer Instructions for Use (IFU) or treating clinician-specific instructions.


### General Principles

- Dressing changes and line care are aseptic procedures requiring documented competency
- Change dressings more frequently if: soiled, damp, loose, or infection suspected
- Use transparent semi-permeable dressings where possible to allow site visualisation
- Document all dressing changes, connector changes, and line flushes in the patient record


### Peripherally Inserted Central Catheter (PICC)

#### Dressing Change

- Interval: Every 7 days, or sooner if dressing is soiled, damp, loose, or if site inspection is required.
- Technique: Surgical-ANTT (sterile gloves, critical aseptic field).
- Skin antisepsis: 2% chlorhexidine in 70% alcohol (unless contraindicated); allow to dry fully (minimum 30 seconds).
- Securement: Use manufacturer-recommended securement device; check position and integrity.
- Documentation: Date of dressing change, dressing type, skin condition, external catheter length (measure and compare to baseline).

#### Connector/Needle-Free Cap Change

- Interval: Every 7 days, or as per manufacturer IFU, or after blood sampling.
- Technique: Standard-ANTT; scrub hub with 70% alcohol or 2% chlorhexidine in 70% alcohol for 15 seconds and allow to dry before removal/connection.

#### Flushing (When Accessed)

- Before use: Assess patency; flush with 10mL 0.9% sodium chloride (prefilled syringe preferred).
- After use: Flush with 10mL 0.9% sodium chloride, then lock as per prescription/IFU (e.g., heparin or saline lock).
- Technique: Positive pressure or neutral technique as per device IFU to prevent blood reflux.

#### When Not in Use (Idle PICC)

Flush weekly with 10mL 0.9% sodium chloride, then lock as prescribed (typically heparin 10 units/mL for valved PICCs; follow IFU).


### Implanted Venous Access Port (PORT / TIVAD)

#### Accessed Port (In Situ Non-Coring Needle)

- Dressing change: Every 7 days, or sooner if soiled, damp, or loose.
- Technique: Surgical-ANTT (sterile gloves, critical aseptic field).
- Skin antisepsis: 2% chlorhexidine in 70% alcohol; allow to dry fully.
- Needle change: Every 7 days, or as per manufacturer IFU. Use new non-coring needle and new dressing.
- Documentation: Date accessed, needle type/length/gauge, skin condition, confirmation of blood return and patency.

#### Connector/Needle-Free Cap Change (Accessed Port)

Interval: Every 7 days, or after blood sampling. Technique: Standard-ANTT; scrub hub 15 seconds with alcohol/chlorhexidine.

#### Flushing (When Accessed)

- Before use: Assess patency; flush with 10mL 0.9% sodium chloride.
- After use: Flush with 10mL 0.9% sodium chloride, then lock with heparin (usually 500 units in 5mL saline unless otherwise prescribed).
- De-access: Flush and lock as above before removing non-coring needle.

#### When Not in Use (De-Accessed Port)

Flush monthly with 10mL 0.9% sodium chloride followed by heparin lock (500 units in 5mL, or as per local protocol/IFU), using a non-coring needle access and de-access procedure.


### Peripheral IV Access (Cannula or Winged Infusion Set)

#### Peripheral IV Cannula

- Routine replacement: Clinically indicated (no fixed interval), or 72-96 hours maximum if no clinical indication for earlier removal.
- Inspect site: At least every shift and before each infusion; check for pain, redness, swelling, phlebitis.
- Dressing: Replace if loose, soiled, or damp; otherwise leave intact to minimise manipulation.
- Removal: At first sign of phlebitis (VIP score ≥2; see Appendix J), infiltration, or when no longer needed.

#### Winged Infusion Set ("Butterfly")

- Use: Short-duration supervised infusions only (typically <4 hours).
- Routine replacement: Single use per access episode; remove immediately after infusion complete.
- Do not: Leave in situ unattended or overnight.
- Monitoring: Continuous or very frequent site observation during infusion (minimum every 15 minutes).

#### Flushing Peripheral Access

- Before use: Flush with 5-10mL 0.9% sodium chloride to confirm patency.
- After use (if maintaining access): Flush with 5-10mL 0.9% sodium chloride; saline lock (no heparin required for peripheral lines).
- Between medications: Flush with 5-10mL 0.9% sodium chloride.


### Troubleshooting: When to Change Dressings/Devices Sooner

#### Change Dressing Immediately If

- Soiled, damp, or blood-stained
- Loose or peeling (exposes insertion site)
- Patient reports pain, tenderness, or discharge at site
- Suspected infection (redness, warmth, swelling, purulent discharge, fever)

#### Change Device/Escalate Immediately If

- Signs of infection - do not re-site; escalate to treating team
- Occlusion that cannot be resolved by troubleshooting (do not force flush)
- Suspected thrombosis (arm swelling, pain, collateral veins for PICC/PORT)
- External catheter length change (PICC migration)
- Damaged catheter or leaking connection
- Phlebitis VIP score ≥2 (peripheral access)


### Evidence Base

- RCN Standards for Infusion Therapy (4th edition, 2016)
- epic3 national evidence-based guidelines for preventing healthcare-associated infections
- Manufacturer IFU for specific devices


## Appendix J - Visual Infusion Phlebitis (VIP) Score

### Purpose

To standardise assessment and grading of phlebitis at peripheral IV access sites, enabling early detection and timely intervention.


### Scope

Use for all peripheral IV cannula and winged infusion set sites. Record VIP score at insertion, then at least once per shift (or before each infusion for winged sets).


### VIP Score and Actions

| VIP Score | Observations | Action Required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VIP 0 No signs of phlebitis | IV site appears healthy. | Continue as per plan; re-assess per schedule. |
| VIP 1 Possible first signs | Slight pain near IV site OR slight redness near IV site. | Re-assess the site within 2-4 hours. Document findings. If score remains 1 or worsens, escalate to VIP 2 actions. |
| VIP 2 Early stage phlebitis | Two of the following are evident: pain at IV site, redness, swelling. | REMOVE the device immediately. Document VIP score, site appearance, actions. Apply warm compress if appropriate. Consider re-siting if IV access still required (different vein, opposite limb if possible). Monitor the site daily until resolved. Complete incident report. |
| VIP 3 Medium stage phlebitis | All of the following are evident: pain along path of cannula, redness, swelling. | REMOVE the device immediately. Escalate to senior clinician/on-call nurse. Consider whether IV access is still essential; if yes, use alternative access (PICC/midline/other limb). Monitor site daily; may require medical review if not improving. Complete incident report. |
| VIP 4 Advanced phlebitis / start of thrombophlebitis | All of: pain along path of cannula, redness, swelling, palpable venous cord. | REMOVE the device immediately. Medical review required same day/urgent (treating clinician or GP/111 as appropriate). Do not re-site peripheral access; escalate to treating team for alternative access plan (PICC/PORT). Monitor site; may require anticoagulation or other medical management. Complete incident report. |
| VIP 5 Advanced thrombophlebitis | All of: pain along path of cannula, redness, swelling, palpable venous cord, pyrexia (fever). | REMOVE the device immediately. URGENT medical review required (same day; consider 999 if systemically unwell/sepsis suspected - see SOP 8). Send cannula tip for microbiology if infection suspected and as per local protocol. Blood cultures if systemically unwell (as directed by treating clinician). Do not re-site peripheral access. Complete incident report and escalate to clinical governance lead. |


### Documentation

Record VIP score in the clinical record at:

- Insertion (baseline: should be VIP 0)
- Each shift or before each infusion
- Any time the patient reports pain or you observe a change
- Removal (record final VIP score and reason for removal)

Include:

- Date/time
- VIP score and specific observations (pain, redness, swelling, cord, fever)
- Actions taken
- Patient education provided (report any pain/change immediately)


### Evidence Base

- Visual Infusion Phlebitis (VIP) score: Developed by Andrew Jackson (1998), validated and widely used in UK NHS and internationally.
- RCN Standards for Infusion Therapy: Recommends routine phlebitis scoring for peripheral access.


### References

- Jackson A. (1998). Infection control: a battle in vein infusion phlebitis. Nursing Times 94(4): 68-71.
- RCN (2016). Standards for Infusion Therapy (4th edition).

- ☐ Emergency contact details and escalation routes confirmed

#### Week 2-4 (Depending on Prior Experience)

- ☐ ANTT competency assessment (Standard and Surgical if applicable)
- ☐ Procedure-specific competency assessments as per role
- ☐ Observed practice and feedback sessions
- ☐ Clinical supervision session with named supervisor

#### Ongoing (First 3 Months)

- ☐ Regular supervision (weekly initially, then monthly)

- ☐ BLS and anaphylaxis training booked and completed
- ☐ Probation review at 3 months with competency portfolio review


### Training Record Management

Training records must include:

- Staff member name and role
- Training module completed
- Date completed
- Expiry/renewal date
- Certificate or competency assessment outcome
- Trainer/assessor name and signature (for practical competencies)

Training records are:

- Stored securely (electronic or paper) in the staff member's personnel file
- Monitored by the clinical governance lead for compliance
- Reviewed during staff appraisal and supervision
- Audited annually to ensure no lapsed mandatory training

#### Lapsed Training

If mandatory training lapses, the staff member must not perform the relevant activity until refresher training is completed. For clinical competencies (e.g., PICC access), a supervised re-assessment may be required before resuming independent practice.


### Competency Assessment and Sign-Off Process

1. Pre-assessment: Staff member confirms training completion. Reviews SOP and equipment. Assessor and staff member agree assessment date.
2. Assessment: Observed practice (real patient or simulation). Assessor uses competency checklist (Appendix D). Staff member demonstrates: knowledge, ANTT, patient communication, documentation, complication management.
3. Outcome: Competent (signed off; authorised for independent practice) OR Not yet competent (further supervised practice and re-assessment).
4. Documentation: Signed competency form filed in staff training record. Competency logged in staff competency register.


### Annual Training Compliance Report

The clinical governance lead will produce an annual training compliance report for the board/governance meeting, including:

- % compliance for each mandatory training module
- Number of staff with lapsed training and remedial actions
- Competency assessment completion rates
- Training needs identified from incidents, complaints, or audits
- Training plan for the following year

Target: 95% compliance for all mandatory training across the workforce.
