Thank you for your time today to discuss your professional development and experience reflections to date working within the service. 

As mentioned prior, this call was a chance for us to discuss 1:1 how you have felt working in the service and with particular clients and give you an opportunity to provide feedback. 

We spoke at 13:00 on the 27th May 2026 by phone. Here is a summary from our call:

Challenges Encountered:
You were clear about the challenges faced working in private homes and how this presents unique challenges compared to hospice or hospital settings. It requires a significant period to build trust and rapport with the patient and their family, particularly when working alone. Managing family expectations and their need for control in their own environment is a complex social situation. This requires substantial mental preparation before each shift, beyond just clinical readiness, to anticipate and handle any situation that may arise.

Professional Growth Areas:
You have developed a greater understanding of adapting to complex social situations, particularly in the home care environment. This experience has improved my ability to deal with the dynamics of working closely with a patient's family. You have also enhanced your documentation skills, learning to create detailed and comprehensive notes that provide a clear nursing impression and overview of the day's events. This process of narrative documentation, rather than just using checklists, has deepened my reflective practice. You feel more confident in your knowledge of medications and my ability to articulate their purpose to families, who may verify the information independently.

Team Collaboration and Communication:
You enjoy working with the current team, finding it to be a good and supportive environment. Communication and collaboration are strong, with staff helping each other, even outside of work hours, to ensure seamless care. The handover process is comprehensive, and the use of group messaging for key updates (e.g., family requests, stock levels, district nurse visits) ensures all team members are on the same page. This creates a perception of a cohesive and well-informed team for patients and families. Collaborating with external teams, like district nurses, has also been a positive learning experience in shared care.

Feedback and Suggestions:
You feel the team is composed of the right people, and the guidance provided from myself is clear and informative. The feedback on documentation and performance is well-organised and helps in professional development. Regarding the documentation software, it would be beneficial if the templates did not default to "doctor's consultation" and allowed for fields not relevant to nursing to be left blank - we discussed that this was a system field that is unavoidable but noted this for our further development of internal systems and training. 

Questions and Concerns:
You had a question regarding the source of patient referrals. It was clarified that referrals are received privately from consultants and organisations like LOC and HCA, rather than from social services or the NHS. You also inquired about the scope of care provided, which includes palliative care but also hospital at home, rehabilitative care, and IV antibiotics etc.

Additional Comments:
I reinforced the importance of taking a "charge nurse" mentality in the home setting, taking responsibility for broader aspects of care such as monitoring and managing stock supplies. It is understood that in this environment, the nursing team functions as the pharmacy and materials department, requiring a proactive mindset to ensure continuity of safe care.
